Use case: KYC onboarding

Ready to onboard? Not until every check is in.

The passport checks out. The selfie matches. Sanctions screening is clear. The account is approved — on a risk score computed without one of its inputs.

Use case: KYC onboarding — 30 seconds
Signed — someone stands behind it Verifiable — you can re-run it yourself Missing — a gap you can see

The situation

A new client is onboarded. An ID provider verifies the passport scan. A liveness vendor matches a selfie at 0.97. A screening service reports no sanctions matches. A risk model scores the applicant low, and the account is opened.

Three vendors, one model, one decision.

What goes wrong without it

Each vendor signed its own result, which is exactly right — none of these can be re-run, so a signature is the only evidence available.

The risk score, though, was supposed to take a proof of address as an input. No such document was ever filed. The score was computed anyway, and the approval rests on it. Nothing in the account record says so.

The chain

Each step references the one before it. Some can be re-run; others carry a signature.

StepWhat it isHow you check it
Passport scanIdentity documentSignature from the ID provider
Selfie matchLiveness check, score 0.97Signature from the vendor
Sanctions checkScreening, 0 matchesSignature from the screener
Proof of addressRequired documentNo document found
Risk scoreModel output, risk lowMissing an input
Open accountThe decisionDepends on the missing step

What the auditor does

  1. Start from the decisionThe account was approved. The approval references a risk score.
  2. Follow the score to its inputsPassport, selfie, sanctions — and a proof of address that is referenced but absent.
  3. Check each vendor signatureThree independent parties, three keys, three claims about their own work.
  4. Note what cannot be re-runNone of these checks are reproducible. Signatures are the only evidence, which is the correct design.
  5. Surface the gap at onboardingNot at the next regulatory review.

What this still cannot tell you

  • A vendor signature says that vendor produced the result. It does not prove the result is correct.
  • Knowing which key signed is not the same as knowing which person or system authorised it.
  • The chain shows an input is missing. Whether the applicant is genuine is still a human judgement.
