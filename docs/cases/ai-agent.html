Use case: AI agent decisions

Trace every AI decision back to its sources.

An agent reads a contract and a policy, compares prices, and recommends a supplier. It cites three sources. One of them cannot be produced.

Use case: AI agent decisions — 30 seconds
Signed — someone stands behind it Verifiable — you can re-run it yourself Missing — a gap you can see

The situation

A procurement agent retrieves a contract with a unit price, and a company policy requiring at least two quotes. It reasons over both, calls a price-comparison tool, and recommends Supplier B.

The recommendation is acted on.

What goes wrong without it

The retrieved documents are signed by their owners, and the model call is signed by the operator that ran it — reasonable, since an LLM call cannot be replayed to the same answer.

But the price comparison cites a live feed, and no record of that feed exists. The agent's output depends on a number nobody can now produce. The recommendation reads as confident; the evidence underneath has a hole in it.

The chain

Each step references the one before it. Some can be re-run; others carry a signature.

What the auditor does

  1. Start from the recommendationChoose Supplier B. It references a price comparison.
  2. Follow the comparison to its inputsA contract, a policy, and a live price feed that was cited but never captured.
  3. Check what was signedThe documents by their owners, the model call by its operator.
  4. Accept what cannot be re-runThe model call is not reproducible. The signature records who ran it, with which model and settings.
  5. See the holeA cited source that cannot be produced is visible as a gap, not buried in a log.

What this still cannot tell you

  • A signature on a model call records that it ran, not that its reasoning was sound.
  • Citing a source is not the same as having used it correctly.
  • The chain shows the evidence is incomplete. Whether Supplier B was the right choice remains a commercial judgement.
