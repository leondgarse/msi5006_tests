Use case: accounts payable

Close the books with evidence, not guesswork.

Month-end. The numbers reconcile, the report is ready, and then somebody asks who approved the payment. The match is sound. The approval is nowhere.

Use case: accounts payable — 30 seconds
Signed — someone stands behind it Verifiable — you can re-run it yourself Missing — a gap you can see

The situation

A supplier sends invoice INV-1042 for S$48,200. A purchase order, PO-7731, authorises up to S$50,000. Extraction reads the invoice, a matching step confirms the two agree, and the figure lands in the Q3 expense report.

Four steps, three systems, two companies.

What goes wrong without it

The invoice and the PO are each signed by the party that issued them, and the match can be recomputed by anyone holding both. That part is solid.

But the payment approval has no signed record behind it. The report says the decision was to proceed — and nothing supports it. Today that surfaces during an audit, months later, as an email search. With a provenance chain it surfaces immediately, as a visible break between the match and the report.

The chain

Each step references the one before it. Some can be re-run; others carry a signature.

StepWhat it isHow you check it
Invoice INV-1042PDF from the supplierSignature from the supplier
OCR extractReading fields off the PDFSignature from the operator that ran it
Purchase order PO-7731Authorisation limit from the buyerSignature from the buyer
Invoice vs PO matchA deterministic comparisonRe-run it — same inputs, same result
Payment approvalA human decisionNo signed record found
Q3 expense reportThe published figureDepends on the missing step

What the auditor does

  1. Check the report you were givenConfirm the figure matches what was published, and that the publisher signed it.
  2. Ask for the inputsThe invoice, the PO, the extraction and the match record — each must match a reference already in the chain.
  3. Re-run the matchInvoice against PO. Either it agrees or it does not; no trust involved.
  4. Check the signaturesThe supplier on the invoice, the buyer on the PO, the operator on the extraction.
  5. Find the breakThe approval has no signed record. The report depends on a step that cannot be shown.

What this still cannot tell you

  • A signature shows which key approved something, not which person held it.
  • Re-running the match proves the comparison was faithful, not that the invoice itself is genuine.
  • A missing approval might exist somewhere unrecorded — the chain shows it is absent from the evidence, not that it never happened.
