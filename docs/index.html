Meta Structured Data

Every number came from somewhere.
MSD remembers where.

Data moves between systems all day — scanned, extracted, transformed, approved, reported. The evidence for how it got there usually does not travel with it. MSD records the connections, so any figure can be traced back to what produced it.

How MSD works — 30 seconds
Signed — someone stands behind it Verifiable — you can re-run it yourself Missing — a gap you can see

The question that is hard to answer

A figure appears in a quarterly report. Somebody asks where it came from. The honest answer usually involves four systems, two vendors and a spreadsheet nobody can find.

The data made it through. The evidence did not.

MSD attaches the trail to the data itself, so the answer travels with the number instead of living in somebody's memory.

Some of it you can check. The rest you take on trust.

This is the distinction that makes a provenance trail useful rather than decorative. Every step in a pipeline is one of two kinds, and they are not equally trustworthy.

Verifiable

You can re-run it

Totalling a column. Converting a currency. Matching an invoice to a purchase order. Same input, same answer, every time.

MSD records the function, the input and the output. You fetch them and run it again.

  • No signature needed
  • Nobody to believe
Attested

Someone signs for it

Reading text off a scan. Asking a model for a judgement. Taking the photo at all. Run it twice and you may get two answers.

So the operator records what went in and what came out, and signs it.

  • The signature is the evidence
  • Trust rests on whose key it is

A real pipeline is always a mixture. Being able to say which parts are checkable and which are merely attested is the point — compare it with a single signature over a finished report, where everything inside is equally opaque.

What you can do with it

Trace a number Follow any output back through every step that produced it.
Check what you were sent Re-run the reproducible steps yourself. Verify signatures on the rest.
See what is absent A missing step shows as a gap in the chain, not as silence.
Prove your own output Hand a counterparty a figure they can verify without trusting your word for it.

Where this is today

MSD is a new and open protocol, and it is early. We would rather say so than imply an adoption story that does not exist yet.

What works today: signing and verifying structured data, content-addressed references between records, and embedding into the documents you already send. An SDK and key tooling are public. What is still being built: the visual tooling that assembles a graph from dropped files automatically.

What it still cannot tell you

  • A signature attributes a claim to a key, not to a person. Connecting a key to a real identity is a separate problem.
  • No amount of checking shows that an extraction read the document correctly. Only looking at the original answers that.
  • Re-running later calculations cannot repair a bad reading at the start — it can only show the calculations were faithful to it.
