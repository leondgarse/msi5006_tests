For auditors, controllers and compliance teams

The data arrives.
The evidence does not.

Can you tell where your data came from, how it changed, or what is missing when you receive a file? Knowing a file is intact is not enough — you need its history.

What that costs

None of this is a controls failure. The invoice was approved, the client was screened, the model was asked. The evidence just lives in four places under three owners.

Audit prep

Assembled, not retrieved

Evidence is gathered retrospectively from people and inboxes, rather than read off the record.

Counterparty

Trust me, or redo it

A figure sent outside the company carries nothing with it. The recipient either takes your word or repeats the work.

Blind spots

Absence looks like presence

A missing approval is indistinguishable from a completed one until somebody goes looking for it.

Two kinds of step

This is what separates a provenance trail worth having from a decorative one. Steps are not equally trustworthy, and the difference is commercial.

Verifiable

They can re-run it

Totalling a column. Converting a currency. Matching an invoice to a purchase order. Same inputs, same answer, always.

The record keeps the calculation, its inputs and its result.

  • No signature needed
  • Nobody has to be believed

Attested

Someone signs for it

Reading figures off a scan. Asking a model to judge. A human approval. Run it twice and the answer may differ.

So the operator records inputs, method and result, and signs.

  • The signature is the evidence
  • Weight depends on whose key
Most of what a finance pipeline does is reproducible. Those steps stop requiring trust at all — which is the genuinely new part.

A real pipeline mixes both. Stating which parts a counterparty can check, and which rest on an attestation, is more useful than one signature over a finished report where everything inside carries the same unexamined weight.

What an auditor does with it

  1. Start from the figureThe published number and the signed statement that came with it.
  2. Request the inputsEach disclosed piece must match a reference already in the evidence. Nothing can be swapped in afterwards.
  3. Re-run what can be re-runThe calculations agree or they do not. No judgement involved.
  4. Check signatures on the restWhich key signed, and was it authorised for that role.
  5. Read the gapsA step with nothing behind it is visible, rather than silent.

What it does not solve

  • A signature attributes a claim to a key, not a person. Linking keys to people is governance, not cryptography.
  • No checking shows that an extraction read the document correctly. Only the original answers that.
  • Re-running later steps cannot repair a bad reading at the start.
  • It records what happened, not whether it was acceptable.

A system claiming to settle all of this would be overselling. Drawing the line precisely is more useful than drawing it generously.
